Our mission

Scribbling is where a child's confidence begins

Chinnara Chitra Chittara exists for one reason: to put a sheet of paper in front of every child in Karnataka and tell them their imagination is worth something.

No child is turned away. There is no audition, no portfolio and no qualifying round. If a child can hold a pencil, they can enter.

300 Little Artist Awards, each named for a legendary artist, alongside 200 Certificate Awards — and every winner is honoured in person, on stage.

LKG to Class 12. Enter as an individual, or let your school send a batch — drawing teachers who bring students are recognised too.

Why drawing

Every invention began as a sketch

Every child is naturally creative. Give a child a pencil and they will fill the page — not because anyone asked them to, but because drawing is how children think out loud. Those first scribbles build observation, confidence and self-expression long before a child can write a sentence.

Chinnara Chitra Chittara sets out to help every child discover that instinct and believe in it. Drawing is not only training for becoming an artist. It builds imagination and problem-solving — the raw material of every profession, whether a child grows up to be an engineer, a doctor, a scientist or a teacher.

The buildings we live in, the products we use and the technologies we depend on all started the same way: as a rough idea, drawn by hand. Creativity is the foundation of invention, and invention is the foundation of everything else.

So we invite every student to take part, show us what they can do, and celebrate it with us at Chitra Kalotsava.

Before the competition

First, we painted a whole village

Bannadamane did not begin with a competition. It began at Neeralagi, a village 18 km from Gadag, where over two new years our artists painted the walls of the houses and then the bullock carts that work the fields. Both were reported across Karnataka.

Guidelines

Read this before you send anything

Twelve rules, in the order they will matter to you.

01 Norms

How winners are chosen

Prizes follow international and academy norms, not traditional first, second and third ranking.

02 Everyone

Every participant is recognised

Whether or not they win, every entrant receives a memento and a special certificate of appreciation.

03 Prizes

How the 500 awards divide

75 awards in each of the four categories make up 300 Little Artist Awards, plus 200 Certificate Awards, judged on skill and talent.

04 Award

What a winner takes home

Every winner receives the full award package — cash prize, cast memento and a personalised certificate. See Awards for the breakdown.

05 Topic

The child chooses the subject

Students are free to draw anything. The work must be the child's own — parents and teachers may guide, but never draw.

06 Medium

Any medium is allowed

Crayon, watercolour, pencil, pen, oil paint, charcoal — or several mixed together. No medium scores higher than another.

07 Surface

What to draw on

Paper or card sheet — A4 (8″ × 12″) or A3 (11″ × 14″) — or canvas.

08 Details

Label the back of the artwork

Write the child's name, class, school and contact number on the reverse, attested with the school's official seal.

09 Sending

Schools send in one parcel

The organising or drawing teacher collects all original artworks, sorts them by category, and sends them together by courier.

10 Refunds

Fees are non-refundable

Once a registration is confirmed it cannot be cancelled, and the registration fee will not be returned.

11 Fee

What it costs to enter

₹150 per entry, plus courier charges. Ask for an official receipt for every entry you submit.

12 Judging

The judges' decision is final

Judging is done by experts in the field. Their decision is final and binding, and they may verify that an entry is genuinely the child's own work.
